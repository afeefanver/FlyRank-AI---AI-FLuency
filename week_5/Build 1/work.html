Mentoring three interns on a live AI product

Past experience, leading three interns.

The problem

Three interns were joining a live product with little experience of language models. Dropping them straight into the codebase risked breaking things and teaching them nothing.

What I did and decided

I put learning before access. Each intern started with small, isolated tasks and reading about how LLMs behave, and only then touched the real code. They worked on three tracks: testing the language model, testing the product, and a capstone AI Note Generator.

What came of it

What each track produced, and one thing an intern could do at the end that they couldn't at the start.

Multi-Modal RAG Question Answering System

The problem

Long reports mix prose and tables, and a plain text splitter loses the structure that makes the numbers meaningful. I wanted a system that could answer questions about a 67-page IMF Article IV report and show where each answer came from.

What I did and decided

I handled text and tables with semantic chunking and indexed 485 chunks with FAISS. Answers are generated with GPT and carry source-level citations, so a reader can check each claim. I kept the cost under $0.10 per session. Stack: Python, LangChain, FAISS, OpenAI API, Streamlit.

What came of it

One real example: a question it answered correctly with its citation, and one it got wrong and why.
